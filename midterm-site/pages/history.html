01 / Roots & meaning

A style that
speaks volumes.

The afro carries many stories. Its meaning shifts across people and generations, but it has long made natural Black hair visible, celebrated, and proudly self-defined.

A short history

Visibility is powerful.

People of African descent have worn and cared for natural hair in many forms across generations and cultures. In the United States, the afro became especially visible during the Black freedom movements of the 1960s and 1970s.

For many, wearing hair naturally was a way to challenge narrow beauty standards and affirm Black identity. The style became associated with pride, solidarity, and the right to define beauty for oneself.

There is no single meaning or single afro. Today, people wear natural hair in ways that reflect their own lives—connecting with history while making something personal and new.

Keep in mind

One style. Many stories.

Hair is personal. Ask people about their own experiences, and let each person decide what their hair means to them.
